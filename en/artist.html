Nagauta shamisen · 30 years

Soichiro Yamashina

Began the shamisen at eight and has played for kabuki theatres in Tokyo and Osaka. Now based in Kobe, moving between the stage and the practice room.

“I want you to take home the moment the sound came from your own hands.”

How did you first meet nagauta?

My grandmother ran a practice studio, so the shamisen was always sounding at home. I still remember how heavy the plectrum felt when I was first allowed to hold it, at eight.

What do you care about most when you play?

On stage, everything is about breathing with the actors. You don't play to stand out; you play to hold the scene up. That is what I care about most.

What would you like travellers to take home?

It doesn't matter if you play perfectly. I want you to take home the feeling of the plectrum striking the string, and the sound coming from you.

Nagauta song & shamisen · 18 years

Chizuru Hayase

Sings and plays. Studied piano at a music college before turning to nagauta, and is known for explaining it side by side with Western music.

“The magic of nagauta lives in the pauses no score can write down.”

How did you first meet nagauta?

I was studying piano when I heard nagauta at the Kabukiza theatre, and the pauses that no score could write down stunned me. I was looking for a teacher that same day.

What do you care about most when you play?

When I compare it with Western music, it clicks for visitors from abroad. Once you hear the differences, the fun of Japanese sound opens up.

What would you like travellers to take home?

Let's sing a little together, too. When the shamisen and your voice meet, nagauta suddenly feels very close.

Nagauta shamisen · 12 years

Ritsu Fujishiro

Discovered nagauta in a university music club and trained seriously after graduating. Teaches in English and has performed abroad.

“Even without a shared language, the sound gets through.”

How did you first meet nagauta?

I heard an older student play in my university club and couldn't believe Japan had music this fast and this cool.

What do you care about most when you play?

Performing abroad, I have seen again and again how sound gets through even without a shared language. So I keep the talking short and let you hear it first.

What would you like travellers to take home?

Ask me anything in English. From holding the shamisen to enjoying kabuki, I'll share things you can use for the rest of your trip.

Nagauta shamisen · 40 years

Haru Onodera

Born in Kobe and raised by a nagauta teacher, Onodera has lived with nagauta for almost half a century, and prefers unhurried sessions over tea in a home studio.

“The shamisen rings best when the heart is calm.”

How did you first meet nagauta?

My mother was a nagauta teacher. I grew up in our house in Kobe, listening to her students practise every day.

What do you care about most when you play?

Not to hurry. We drink tea, talk about the season, and only then pick up the shamisen. It rings best when the heart is calm.

What would you like travellers to take home?

Spend a slow hour with me in Kobe. I'd be happy if one hour of your trip became one you remember.
